# Honest context — Mirela Ionescu

*This file is private. It never gets sent to an employer. Its whole job is to stop the
agent producing generic, cheerful output that doesn't match my actual situation.*

Last updated: 2026-08-30

## Where I actually am

I have been at Halcyon Retail Group for nearly five years and the interesting work
finished about eighteen months ago. The platform is stable, which is the problem — I am
maintaining pipelines I built in 2022 and the roadmap for 2027 is "keep the lights on and
cut cloud spend by 15%." I am not miserable. I am bored, and being bored for another year
would cost me the ability to move at a senior level at all.

I am not desperate. I have a job, I can be patient, and I would rather stay put than take
a sideways move into another maintenance role with a better title.

## Visa — the hard constraint

I am Romanian, living in Manchester. I came on a Student visa in 2020 for an MSc, moved to
a Graduate visa, and Halcyon sponsored my Skilled Worker visa in August 2023. It runs to
August 2028.

**Any new employer must hold a UK sponsor licence and be willing to issue a Certificate of
Sponsorship.** This is not a preference. If a company cannot sponsor, the conversation is
over regardless of how good the role is, and I would rather find that out in week one than
after three interview rounds. I have already wasted six weeks on a company that said
"we're open to sponsorship" and meant "we've never done it and won't start."

Check the gov.uk register of licensed sponsors before I invest time. Being on the register
is necessary but not sufficient — the licence has to cover the Skilled Worker route and
they have to actually be willing.

I do not need a salary high enough for the going-rate threshold to be a problem at senior
level, but it is worth sanity-checking anything under £50k.

## Money

Currently on £68,000 plus a bonus that has paid out around £3,000 the last two years. Call
it £71k total.

My floor is **£75,000 base**. Below that a move is not worth the disruption and the visa
risk. I will not say this number first in a conversation, but I will not pretend the floor
is softer than it is either. £85k+ and I stop caring about most other things.

## What I want next

- Work on a platform that is still being built, not one being maintained.
- Streaming and near-real-time, not another batch warehouse. I have done batch to death.
- Some ownership of technical direction. Not management — I do not want to manage people
  in the next two years and I should stop letting interviewers talk me into saying I do.
- A team of more than three engineers. I have been the "data person" and it is lonely and
  bad for my growth.

## What I do not want

- Any role that is 60%+ dbt model maintenance dressed up as platform work.
- Consultancy or client-facing delivery work. I did this at Vantiq and I was unhappy.
- Full return-to-office. I go in two days a week now and that is my ceiling — I live 40
  minutes out and I am not moving.
- Companies where the data team reports into Finance. Twice now, and both times the work
  became month-end reporting.
- Crypto, gambling, defence. Not negotiable, not up for a discussion about nuance.

## Weaknesses I should stop hiding from myself

- **Kubernetes is thin.** I can read a manifest and I have deployed to a cluster somebody
  else ran. I have never operated one. Job descriptions asking for "strong Kubernetes" are
  a genuine gap, not a modesty problem, and I should not let a résumé bullet imply
  otherwise.
- **No large-scale streaming in production.** I have run Kafka as a transport layer. I
  have not built or owned a Flink or Spark Streaming system serving live traffic. This is
  the exact thing I want to move into, which means I am applying to roles I am not yet
  obviously qualified for, and I need the story about that to be honest rather than
  bluffed.
- **My Spark is 2022-era.** I have not kept up.
- I interview badly in the first twenty minutes. I warm up. I need the small talk and the
  "tell me about yourself" answer rehearsed to the point of being automatic, because that
  is where I lose people.
- English is my third language. It is fluent — I did a master's in it and I work in it
  every day — but under pressure my sentences get long and formal, and written English I
  have not edited reads stiff. Anything drafted for me should use plain, simple sentences.
  If a cover letter sounds like a native speaker showing off, it does not sound like me.
