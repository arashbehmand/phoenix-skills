# What to go after in a roast

The checklist. Not every item applies to every résumé; work through it and quote the lines
that do.

## Claims that will not survive an interview

- **A number that cannot be defended.** For every figure, ask: does the candidate know how
  it was measured, over what period, against what baseline? If the honest answer is "that
  sounded about right", it is a liability. Flag it and ask for the real one or cut it.
- **A suspiciously round or extreme number.** "Improved performance 40x", "saved
  $2,000,000", "led a team of 50". Real numbers are usually untidy. An extreme one with no
  context reads as invented even when it is true — it needs the context or it goes.
- **A verb the rest of the résumé does not support.** "Architected", "owned", "drove" on
  one line while every other line for the same role says "supported" and "contributed to".
- **A skill listed but never used in a bullet.** If it is real, it belongs in an
  achievement. If it only appears in the skills block, a reader assumes it is aspirational.

## Claims with nothing behind them

- "Picks up new tools quickly", "go-to person for hard problems", "passionate about clean
  code", "thrives in ambiguity". Each one needs replacing with the single instance that
  proves it, or cutting.
- An objective statement — "Seeking a challenging role that leverages…". Dead weight.
- Generic praise of a technology or a methodology. Says nothing about the candidate.

## Packaging problems

- **A headline or summary that is a keyword list.** "Senior Data Engineer | Python | Spark
  | AWS | Kafka | dbt | Airflow | SQL". Reads as stuffing to a person and is penalised by
  the parser.
- **Bullets that bury the result.** The outcome should be the visible part, near the
  front, not the last clause.
- **Uniform bullets.** Every one opening "Built / Developed / Led / Implemented", every one
  the same length. Real writing varies.
- **A three-line bullet.** That is two bullets, or one that needs cutting.
- **The AI tells.** Em-dashes everywhere, tricolons in every sentence, "spearheaded",
  "leveraged", "seamless", a uniform paragraph rhythm. Roughly one recruiter in five now
  bins a résumé that reads as generated.

## Things a reader pauses at

- Unexplained short tenures — under a year, more than once.
- A title that undersells the work, or oversells it.
- A very long tenure with no visible progression.
- A gap, or something that reads as a gap on a skim.
- An employer nobody recognises, with no one-line description.
- Filler from more than about eight years ago that pushes the recent work down the page.

## Calibration

After the line-by-line, step back: is this résumé aimed at the right level? Someone with a
staff-level record applying to senior roles, or a mid-level record aimed two rungs up, is a
finding in itself — and it is a `aligning-career-targets` conversation, not a wording fix.
