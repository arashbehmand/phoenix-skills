# The hard-question playbook

The questions almost every candidate dreads, and how to build an answer that is true,
short, and does not sound defensive. The raw material is in `profile/honest-context.md` —
this is how to turn the real reason into a sayable one.

## The shape of a good answer

1. **One or two sentences, then stop.** The dread makes people over-explain, and the
   over-explaining is what sounds like a problem. Say the thing, then be quiet.
2. **Forward-facing.** End on what the candidate wants now, not on what went wrong.
3. **No blame, no apology.** "The team reorganised and my role narrowed" is a fact. "My
   manager was difficult" is a red flag to the interviewer even when it is true.
4. **Consistent with the file.** The answer cannot contradict what
   `honest-context.md`, the résumé, or `resume-notes.md` say. If the honest reason is one
   the candidate has decided to keep private, the answer is still true, just less complete
   — never invented.

## The recurring set

**"Why did you leave / why are you leaving?"**
The real reason is in honest-context, area 1. Translate it to the sayable version: what
finished, and what the candidate wants next that this role has. "The platform went into
maintenance and I want to build again" is enough. Do not narrate the disillusionment.

**"There's a gap on your CV."**
Name the period plainly and say what it was, in one sentence. If it was a deliberate break,
say so without justifying it. If it was a hard time, "I took some time out and I'm ready to
go again" closes it. What not to do: a fabricated project or a fake company to paper over
it — that unravels in a reference check and it is worse than the gap.

**"That's a lot of short stints."**
Group them: "three contract roles in two years, all delivery work, all completed." Then the
turn: what the candidate wants now is the opposite of that — one place, longer, more
ownership. The pattern stops being a worry once it has a stated reason and a stated end.

**"Why have you been here so long without a promotion?"**
The honest answer is usually in area 1 or area 7. "The work was good until about eighteen
months ago, and I stayed longer than I should have" is honest and disarming. Follow with
what changed and why now.

**"What's your salary expectation?"**
Straight from honest-context, area 3, exactly and without hedging. Give the floor as a
floor if asked directly; do not soften it to seem easy. If the file says not to name a
number first, the answer is a range with the floor at the bottom, or "what's the band for
this role?".

**"Are you authorised to work here?"**
Straight from honest-context. The specific status and what the employer would need to do,
in one sentence. Not "it's complicated" — the specifics ("Skilled Worker visa to 2028,
you'd need to hold a sponsor licence and issue a CoS") are reassuring; vagueness is not.

**"What's your biggest weakness?"**
One real one from honest-context, area 5 or 7, that is not disqualifying for this role,
plus the specific thing being done about it. Not a strength in disguise. If Kubernetes is
thin and the role is not Kubernetes-heavy, that is a fine answer.

**"Where do you see yourself in five years?"**
The primary track from `career-plan.md`, in the candidate's words. Concrete enough to sound
real, not so concrete it sounds like it excludes the job on offer.

## Rehearsal note

These get written into section 4 talking points where they map to a requirement, and
flagged in section 2 as questions to expect. The candidate should say each one out loud in
a mock before the interview — the first twenty minutes is where these land or do not, and
reading is not the same as saying.
