# The cover letter

Output: `applications/<slug>/cover-letter.md`.

## The constraints

**Three paragraphs, maximum. Around 700 words, hard ceiling — and much shorter is better.**
Two good paragraphs beat three padded ones. The worked example in the phoenix-skills
repository is 360 words.

**Simple language.** This is the most-ignored instruction and the one that matters most.
Phoenix's original wording: *keep the language grammatically correct, professional and
clean, but bear in mind the applicant is a non-native speaker, so to have a true tone keep
the language simple and avoid complex jargon or corporate jargon.* Aim at roughly IELTS
band 7 — fluent, correct, unshowy.

The reason is not readability. It is that the letter has to sound like the person who then
turns up to the call. A letter full of elegant subordinate clauses, written for a candidate
whose spoken English is good but plain, creates a gap the interviewer notices. Short
sentences. Ordinary words. No "leverage", "synergy", "passionate about", "thrilled to",
"dynamic environment".

**The goal is an interview, not the job.** Phoenix's line: *the goal is to maximise chances
of getting an interview, not to get the offer without interview.* The letter does not have
to prove the candidate is the best applicant. It has to make someone want a conversation.
That permits leaving something interesting unsaid, and it rules out the exhaustive
paragraph listing every qualification.

**Honest excitement.** Interest in the role and the fit, stated in a way the candidate
would actually say out loud. If the honest context says the main draw is the salary and
the commute, do not manufacture a passion for the mission — write about the work instead.

## Structure

**Start with a salutation.** `Dear Hiring Manager,` — or a name, when one is known from
the posting or from `contacts.md`. A name is worth using.

**Do not write a letterhead.** No date, no address block, no contact details, no subject
line. `cover-letter.md` holds the letter *body*. The candidate's contact details are on
the résumé and in the application form; repeating them here means maintaining them twice.

**The sign-off is a judgment call.** Phoenix's prompt forbade one, because its PDF template
appended `Sincerely, <name>` automatically. There is no template here, so:

- Pasting into an email or a form's free-text box → add `Sincerely,` and the name.
- Rendering through something that adds one → leave it off.

Store the body without it, and add it when sending. Say which you did.

**Plain Markdown, paragraphs separated by blank lines.** No headings, no bullet lists. It
is a letter.

## What goes in the three paragraphs

There is no fixed formula, but this shape works and is what the example follows:

1. **Why this role, concretely.** Open with something true and specific — a detail from
   the posting, a problem the company has that the candidate has solved before. Then the
   strongest evidence from `fit.md` section 4, with its numbers. Never open with "I am
   writing to express my interest in".
2. **The honest middle.** Where the candidate is strong, and — when they have decided to
   be upfront — where they are not. A gap named plainly and paired with what the candidate
   does bring is far stronger than a gap the reader finds themselves in round two. Check
   `fit.md` section 5 and `honest-context.md`; this is a decision the candidate makes, not
   one to make for them.
3. **The close.** Why it matters to them, briefly, and ideally a real question about the
   work. A question shows the candidate has thought about the job rather than the
   application.

Reference specific skills and experience from the résumé. Generic praise of the company is
worth nothing; it is available to every other applicant.

## Use the rest of the workspace

- **`fit.md`** — section 5 says which strength to lead with and which gap to address.
  Use it. It is why the letter is not generic.
- **`research/<company>.md`** — one specific, verified fact about the company is worth
  more than three sentences of admiration. Do not use an estimate as if it were a fact.
- **`contacts.md`** — if there has been an exchange, reference it and match its tone. Do
  not write to a stranger someone has already spoken to.
- **`preferences.md`** — standing instructions live here. Follow them.

## Do not

- Repeat the résumé in prose. The reader has both.
- Explain what the company does back to the company.
- Use "As a seasoned professional", "I believe I would be a great fit", "Thank you for
  your time and consideration" as a paragraph of its own.
- Claim anything the tailored résumé does not support.
- Exceed three paragraphs, whatever the reason.

## Before it is sent

Read it back to the candidate. It goes out under their name and in their voice, and they
are the only one who can say whether it sounds like them. Do not send anything on someone's
behalf unless you were asked to.
