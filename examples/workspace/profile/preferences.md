# Preferences — Mirela Ionescu

*Standing instructions for how output should be produced. Prose, not a form — Phoenix
learned that a preferences schema goes stale faster than a paragraph does.*

Last updated: 2026-08-30

## Résumé

Two pages, and I would rather cut a job than shrink the font. Halcyon and Brightpath get
the space; Vantiq can be three lines. Anything before 2016 is a single line under
Education and nothing more.

Lead with the platform work, not the modelling. Every version of my CV that led with dbt
got me interviews for analytics engineering roles I did not want.

Keep the numbers. If a bullet has no number in it, it is either a bullet worth deleting or
a bullet I need to go and find the number for — ask me, do not invent one.

Do not add a "Skills" wall of eighty keywords. Group them and keep each group to things I
would be happy to be interviewed on for thirty minutes.

Do not use the word "spearheaded". Ever.

## Cover letters

Short. Three paragraphs, and I would rather have two good ones than three padded ones.

Plain English. Short sentences. I am a fluent non-native speaker and a letter full of
elegant subordinate clauses does not sound like the person who then turns up to the call.

No "I am writing to express my enthusiastic interest in". Start with something true and
specific about the role or the company.

The goal is to get to an interview, not to win the job on paper. It is fine to leave
something interesting unsaid.

## Screening questions

Three to five sentences each. Real examples with real numbers. If an honest answer to a
question is weak, give me the weak honest answer and flag it — I would rather rewrite it
myself than send something invented.

## Tone in messages to recruiters

Warm, brief, not eager. I reply within a day. I do not use exclamation marks and I do not
open with "Hope you're doing well!". If a message needs to say no, it should say no in the
first two sentences and be kind after that, not before.

I want to be asked before anything is sent on my behalf. Draft it, show me, I will send it.

## Research

I care about, in this order: does the company hold a sponsor licence, is it solvent, is
the data team more than three people, and what do the last twelve months of reviews say
about management churn. Funding-round trivia is the least useful thing you can tell me.

Say when something is an estimate. An estimated revenue figure labelled as an estimate is
useful; the same figure stated flatly is worse than nothing because I might repeat it in
an interview.

## Interview prep

I want the twenty questions and I want them harsh. Do not soften a question because it
would be uncomfortable — being asked "why have you stayed five years without a promotion?"
for the first time in the room is much worse than reading it here.

Flag anything you inferred rather than knew. If you do not have the interviewer's
background, say so; do not guess what they will care about.
