## 2. Business overview and company phase

Kestrel Labs sells carbon-accounting infrastructure for road and sea freight. The product
is an API: customers send shipment and telematics data, and get back emissions figures
calculated to a published methodology, with an audit trail they can hand to a regulator or
to their own customers.

**Phase: growth-stage, post-product-market-fit.** Series B, roughly 140 employees, selling
to enterprise rather than SMB, with named reference customers. Not a startup looking for a
market and not yet a mature company.

Customers, per their own site and press coverage: several UK and EU freight operators and
"two of the four largest UK grocery retailers." The grocer relationships are the credible
signal — freight emissions are those customers' Scope 3, which is where the reporting
pressure actually sits.

**Demand driver:** CSRD in the EU and UK reporting requirements make this a compliance
purchase rather than a sustainability nice-to-have. That matters for stability — budgets
survive downturns better when the line item is "regulatory" than when it is "ESG."

**Competitors:** several venture-funded emissions-accounting platforms, plus the
sustainability modules of the large ERP and TMS vendors. The ERP threat is the structural
one: if a freight customer's existing TMS ships adequate emissions reporting, the
best-of-breed API has a harder sale. Worth asking about.

## 4. Culture and employee satisfaction

**Caveat up front: the review sample is small.** 31 Glassdoor reviews for a 140-person
company is thin enough that two disgruntled leavers move the average. Read directionally.

- **Glassdoor: 4.1 / 5** across 31 reviews. Recommend-to-a-friend 82%. CEO approval 91%
  (small sample).
- Sub-ratings: work-life balance 4.3, compensation and benefits 3.7, senior management 3.6,
  career opportunities 3.4.
- **Recurring positives:** the six-week cycle with a two-week cooldown is mentioned
  approvingly in several reviews, specifically that cooldowns are protected rather than
  quietly filled with work. Engineers describe unusual autonomy over technical decisions.
  Multiple reviews mention that the mission is a real retention factor.
- **Recurring negatives:** process immaturity — "we're still figuring out how to run
  projects at this size" appears in three reviews from 2025-2026. Career progression is the
  weakest sub-rating and reviews say levelling is informal. Compensation described as fair
  but not market-leading.
- **Indeed:** 3.9 / 5, 12 reviews. Consistent with the above.

## 5. Red flags

Assessed against layoffs, turnover, management quality, and litigation.

- **Layoffs:** none found. No entry on layoffs.fyi, no press coverage, no LinkedIn activity
  suggesting a reduction.
- **Turnover:** ⚠️ **Two engineering leads appear to have left in Q1 2026.** This is
  inferred from LinkedIn profile changes, not from any Kestrel statement — it is a weaker
  source than the rest of this document and should be treated as such. Two departures at
  lead level in one quarter, in a nine-person platform team, is worth a question. It is not
  by itself evidence of a problem: people leave after a Series B vests, and both moved to
  larger companies rather than out of the industry.
- **Management reviews:** senior management at 3.6 is the second-weakest sub-rating but is
  not in the range that signals a real problem. No reviews naming individuals negatively.
- **Litigation:** nothing found in UK court listings or press.
- **Accounts / solvency:** clean, per Companies House above.
- **Structural risk, not a flag:** single-product company in a market where large ERP and
  TMS incumbents could bundle. Normal for the stage.

**Overall: no disqualifying red flags.** The lead departures are the one thing to ask about.

## 7. Interview process and difficulty

The job description publishes the process, which is itself a positive signal:

1. 30-minute recruiter intro call
2. 60-minute technical conversation, two platform engineers, systems design, no live coding
3. Take-home (3 hours, **paid**) *or* a walkthrough of prior work — candidate's choice
4. 45 minutes with the Head of Platform
5. 30 minutes with the Emissions Science lead

Stated target: three weeks end to end.

**Glassdoor interview reports (7 reviews, so thin):** difficulty rated 3.4/5. Described as
"design-heavy, no leetcode." Two reviewers specifically praised being offered the
prior-work walkthrough instead of a take-home. One negative report concerned a slow gap
between stages 3 and 4.

Paying for the take-home and offering an alternative to it are both meaningful — they are
what a company does when it has thought about candidate time.
